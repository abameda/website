Selected work / Case study 04

Yosr — learning that speaks Arabic first.

An Arabic-first learning management system for students and instructors: authentication and roles, course and lesson architecture, payments, and secure video delivery — built as a product architecture meant to grow.

CategoryWeb platform · Education
RoleProduct architecture · Full-stack engineering · UX design
StackNext.js · Supabase · Prisma · TypeScript
Timelineadd dates
01

Context

Most learning platforms treat Arabic as a translation target: an LTR product mirrored at the last minute, with Arabic text squeezed into layouts designed for English. Yosr starts from the opposite premise — an educational platform where Arabic is the native language of the interface, the content, and the design decisions.

02

Business problem

  • Instructors need a serious platform: course management, enrolment, payments, and control over who can watch their content.
  • Paid video content must actually be protected — casual downloading and link-sharing directly threaten the business model.
  • The platform had to launch lean but be architected for growth in courses, students, and instructors.
03

User problem

A student needs a distraction-free path: find the course, pay once, and continue exactly where they left off — in an interface that reads naturally in Arabic. An instructor needs to publish and organise content, see their students, and trust that paid lessons stay behind the paywall.

04

Constraints

  • RTL-first everything: layout, typography, iconography direction, and component behavior.
  • Video delivery secure enough to protect revenue without punishing legitimate students.
  • A lean stack a small team can operate — no infrastructure that needs its own team.
05

My role

I designed and built the platform: the data model and role system, the Arabic-first interface, the enrolment and payment flows, and the secure video delivery pipeline — full-stack ownership from schema to screen.

06

Product strategy

The strategy: be a product, not a plugin. Rather than assembling disconnected tools — a video host here, a payment link there — Yosr unifies identity, enrolment, payment, and playback into one coherent system, so access control can actually be enforced and the student experience stays seamless.

Browse courses → Enrol & pay → Access granted by role → Watch — securely streamed → Progress saved

Enrolment, payment, and playback governed by one access model.

07

UX & system architecture

  • Role-driven surfaces — students, instructors, and administrators see different products on the same data model.
  • Course architecture — courses → sections → lessons, with enrolment state controlling access at every level.
  • Continue-watching as the home screen — the platform optimises for returning students, because learning is a habit, not a transaction.

08

Design decisions

Arabic typography led the design system: type scale, line heights, and component spacing were tuned for Arabic script first, then verified for Latin. Navigation, progress, and video controls follow RTL reading logic naturally. The result is an interface Arabic-speaking students don't have to mentally translate.

09

Technical implementation

  • Next.js + TypeScript app with server-enforced access control on every content route.
  • Prisma schema modelling users, roles, courses, enrolments, payments, and progress as one typed graph.
  • Supabase for authentication, Postgres, and protected storage — signed, expiring access to video instead of public URLs.
  • Payment integration tied directly to enrolment state, so access is granted and revoked by the system, not by hand.
10

Challenges & trade-offs

Protection vs. playback experience. Perfect content protection doesn't exist; hostile-proof DRM would have hurt every legitimate student. The trade: signed, expiring, identity-bound access that defeats casual sharing while keeping playback instant.

Scaling ambition vs. launch scope. The architecture supports growth — but the launch stayed lean. Designing the data model for the future while building only the present took restraint.

11

Outcome

Qualitative outcomes — stated honestly:

Genuinely Arabic-firstAn LMS designed in Arabic, not translated into it.

Revenue-safe contentPaid lessons stay behind identity-bound, expiring access.

Room to growThe typed data model absorbs new courses, roles, and features without rework.

12

Lessons learned

"Arabic-first" is an architecture decision, not a CSS property. Direction, typography, and language shape the data model, the components, and the defaults — and retrofitting any of it later costs more than starting there. The same lesson generalises: whatever your product's core promise is, build it into the foundation, not the paint.
